Natural Satellites of the Solar System

There are over 200 known moons in our solar system orbiting six of the eight major planets.

Note: that all photos shown are link and other photos is downloaded from NASA

1. Mercury & Venus

Total Moons: 0

Mercury and Venus are the only two planets in our solar system that have no natural moons due to their proximity to the Sun's strong gravitational pull.

2. Earth's Moon (Luna)

Photo courtesy of: NASA SCIENCE

Total Moons: 1

The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. It stabilizes Earth's axial wobble, creating a stable climate, and causes ocean tides.

3. Moons of Mars (Phobos & Deimos)

Photo courtesy of: NASA SCIENCE

Total Moons: 2

Mars has two small, irregularly shaped moons named Phobos and Deimos, which are believed to be captured asteroids.

4. Moons of Jupiter (Galilean Moons)

Photo courtesy of: NASA SCIENCE

Total Moons: 95+

Jupiter's four largest moons are known as the Galilean moons: Io (volcanic), Europa (subsurface ice ocean), Ganymede (largest moon in the solar system), and Callisto.

5. Moons of Saturn (293 Confirmed Moons)

Photo courtesy of: NASA SCIENCE

Total Moons: 290+

Saturn has the most moons of any planet. Its largest, Titan, has a dense atmosphere and methane lakes, while Enceladus shoots water ice plumes into space from an underground ocean.

6. Moons of Uranus (Titania)

Photo courtesy of: NASA SCIENCE

Total Moons: 28

Uranus has 28 known moons, named after characters from the works of William Shakespeare and Alexander Pope. The largest is Titania, followed by Oberon, Umbriel, Ariel, and Miranda.

7. Moons of Neptune (Triton)

Photo courtesy of: NASA SCIENCE

Total Moons: 16

Neptune's largest moon, Triton, orbits the planet in the opposite direction of Neptune's rotation (retrograde orbit) and features active nitrogen geysers.
